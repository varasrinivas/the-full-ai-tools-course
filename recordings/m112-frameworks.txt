Methodology module: no headless artifact (like M-11.1). The record is this dated,

CURRENCY CORRECTION = THE MODULE'S SPINE (a stale-framing catch)
  DORA has moved; using the old framing would be teaching 2023:
   - DORA "de-acronymed" (stands on its own now); the annual report was RENAMED from
     "Accelerate State of DevOps" to "State of AI-assisted Software Development."
   - The metrics went from FOUR to FIVE.
   - MTTR was RENAMED "failed deployment recovery time."
   - THE AI FINDING FLIPPED between 2024 and 2025:
       2024: AI hurt BOTH throughput AND stability.
       2025 (CURRENT, use this): AI HELPS throughput but STILL HURTS stability.

THE FIVE DORA METRICS (current, verbatim, dora.dev/guides/dora-metrics + /four-keys, 2026-07-25)
  SPEED / throughput:
   - Deployment frequency — "The number of deployments over a given period or the time between deployments."
   - Change lead time — "The amount of time it takes for a change to go from committed to version control to deployed in production."
   - Failed deployment recovery time — "The time it takes to recover from a deployment that fails and requires immediate intervention." (this REPLACED MTTR)
  STABILITY / instability:
   - Change fail rate — "The ratio of deployments that require immediate intervention following a deployment."
   - Deployment rework rate (NEW 5th) — "The ratio of deployments that are unplanned but happen as a result of an incident in production."
  (Module presents them split speed vs stability — the durable axis — and flags the 5th as newest.)

WHAT AI CHANGES — 2025 DORA (primary, cloud.google.com/blog/.../announcing-the-2025-dora-report + dora.dev/insights/balancing-ai-tensions, 2026-07-25)
   - "Unlike last year, we observe a positive relationship between AI adoption on both software delivery throughput and product performance."
   - "However, AI adoption does continue to have a negative relationship with software delivery stability."
   - "AI accelerates software development, but that acceleration can expose weaknesses downstream. Without robust control systems, like strong automated testing, mature version control practices, and fast feedback loops, an increase in change volume leads to instability."
   - AMPLIFIER (the module's money quote): "AI doesn't fix a team; it amplifies what's already there. Strong teams use AI to become even better and more efficient. Struggling teams will find that AI only highlights and intensifies their existing problems."
   - Adoption-vs-delivery WEDGE (kills vanity metrics with primary data): "90% of survey respondents report using AI at work." / "More than 80% believe it has increased their productivity." / "30% report little or no trust in the code generated by AI." — coexisting with the negative stability relationship above.
  2024 (superseded, kept only for contrast): "AI adoption significantly increases individual productivity, flow, and job satisfaction. However, it also negatively impacts software delivery stability and throughput." (dora.dev/research/2024/dora-report)
  HEDGE: the widely-circulated 2024 "25% adoption -> ~1.5% throughput / ~7.2% stability" numbers are SECONDARY only (redmonk/cusy) — NOT quoted in the module. The 2025 finding is given directionally (as DORA gives it), not as a single headline %.

SPACE (five dimensions + single-metric thesis)
   - Hard primary quote used (Microsoft Research page): developer productivity "is about more than an individual's activity levels or the efficiency of the engineering systems relied on to ship software, and it cannot be measured by a single metric or dimension."
   - Five dims: Satisfaction & well-being; Performance; Activity; Communication & collaboration; Efficiency & flow.
   - HEDGE: the sharper "(or metric!)" and "never use activity metrics in isolation" lines are attributed to the ACM SPACE paper but only confirmable via SECONDARY today (ACM 403). Module leans on the solid primary abstract quote above; SIM-21's activity-card rationale paraphrases the in-isolation point rather than quoting it as verbatim.

DX CORE 4 (getdx.com/research/measuring-developer-productivity-with-the-dx-core-4, 2026-07-25)
   - "The DX Core 4 encapsulates DORA, SPACE, and DevEx and includes four dimensions": "speed, effectiveness, quality, and business impact."
   - Balance: "changes to one dimension, such as speed, may negatively affect others (e.g., quality or effectiveness)."
   - No single headline metric by design (weights Diffs-per-Engineer against the DXI survey index; "diffs per engineer, requires caution"). NOTE: "DiffDelta" is NOT a Core 4 term — did not use it.
