THE SECOND DIAL — EFFORT (this is what makes it a 2026 policy, not a 2024 one)
=========================================================================
`output_config: {effort: "low"|"medium"|"high"|"xhigh"|"max"}` — GA, no beta header, default
`high`. Claude Code exposes it as `/effort`. Per the skill's guidance:
  - "use a minimum of `high` for intelligence-sensitive work, `max` when correctness matters
    more than cost, and `low` for subagents or simple tasks"
  - "lower effort means fewer and more-consolidated tool calls, less preamble, and terser
    confirmations (`high` is often the sweet spot balancing quality and token efficiency)"
  - Model-choice guidance (code.claude.com/docs/en/costs, l41 sheet): "Sonnet handles most
    coding tasks well and costs less than Opus. Reserve Opus for complex architectural
    decisions or multi-step reasoning. ... For simple subagent tasks, specify `model: haiku`
    in your subagent configuration."
=> A big/small-model policy written in 2026 has TWO dials, not one: WHICH MODEL and HOW MUCH
   EFFORT. Effort is the finer-grained one, it does not change the model's judgment ceiling,
   and lowering it on a capable model is frequently the better move than routing the task to
   a weaker one. Leaders who only debate model tier are arguing about the narrow dial.
   (Thinking tokens bill as output tokens, so effort moves real money.)
CACHE ECONOMICS (same source, and it CONFIRMS M-8.2's measured finding): cache reads cost
  ~0.1x base input; cache writes cost 1.25x (5-min TTL) or 2x (1-hour TTL). "Any byte change
  anywhere in the prefix invalidates everything after it." => this is precisely why pasting a
  file cost 9.7x: it forced cache CREATION (103,192 tokens) instead of cache reads.

=========================================================================
THE THREE BANDS (the lab's structure)
=========================================================================
BAND 1 — STEP (seats). Capitation. Forecast = seats x headcount. Predictable, moves only when
  you hire. NOT a usage control.
BAND 2 — SLOPE (usage). Fee-for-service. Forecast = active developers x active days/month x
  cost per active day.
  *** THE DENOMINATOR THAT MATTERS IS ACTIVE DAYS, NOT HEADCOUNT. *** A seat nobody uses costs
  the step and nothing else.
  PUBLIC PRIOR (Anthropic, code.claude.com/docs/en/costs, verified 2026-07-29, verbatim):
    "Across enterprise deployments, the average cost is around $13 per developer per active
     day and $150-250 per developer per month, with costs remaining below $30 per active day
     for 90% of users."
  AND THE METHOD, which is this course's own advice in the vendor's mouth:
    "To estimate spend for your own team, start with a small pilot group and use the tracking
     tools below to establish a baseline before wider rollout."  (= L-2.1 pilot + M-11.1
     baseline). A prior you never replace is a guess with a citation.
BAND 3 — LUMPY (everything not per-person). THIS IS WHERE BUDGETS BREAK, and it scales with
  POLICY, not headcount:
  - PR review on every push x number of pushes, at $15-25/review (Claude Code Code Review,
    verified 2026-07-28, l33 sheet). "Reviewing on every push runs the most reviews and costs
    the most."
  - Automation / headless runs don't sleep (M-10.4's background agent: $0.2320, 9 turns, 24s).
  - Scheduled tasks fire on their interval "even while the session is idle, sending your full
    context each time."
  - Agent teams: vendor-stated "approximately 7x more tokens than standard sessions when
    teammates run in plan mode" — confirming M-9.5's measured "you pay for every child."
  - Background/idle usage: "typically under $0.04 per session" (small, but non-zero, per session).
  => Every one of these is something a leader switched on in L-03. Budget and policy are one
     conversation.

=========================================================================
THE MODEL POLICY — ROUTE ON RISK, NOT COST
=========================================================================
THE ROUTING TEST [M-8.4, re-read at source m84-model-economics.txt:51-60, verbatim]:
  "The tell is not task SIZE, it is whether you can CHECK the answer. A grind answer you can
   verify in seconds; a judgment answer you cannot verify without redoing the judgment, which
   is why you bought the model in the first place."
  ESCALATION RULE, verbatim: "escalate on failure or on any task whose output you cannot
   verify. Escalation must be triggered by the TASK TYPE, not by the model's confidence — the
   small model was maximally confident while being wrong."
THE MEASURED CAUTIONARY CASE [M-8.4]: on the threshold question the small model answered for
  **$0.0368 and was WRONG** — it asserted the inclusive rule, citing javadoc + constant +
  decision block by line number, while line 99 was strict `>`, and flagged NO discrepancy.
  The large model led with "the repo contradicts itself" + 4 sources + the introducing commit.
  Failure mode is **fluency, not stupidity**. Cheap-and-wrong is the expensive outcome.
ON GRIND WORK [M-8.4]: both models answered 13; small $0.0912 vs large $0.1612 = only **1.8x**,
  because both did the same 4 turns and carried context doesn't get cheaper when the model does.
WHY A COST-MOTIVATED POLICY IS OPTIMIZING THE WRONG TERM [M-8.1]: in one metered invocation
  that used two models, haiku was **$0.000616 = 0.22%** of the bill and opus **99.78%**.
=> Write the policy for CORRECTNESS. It touches cost only incidentally — and the arithmetic
   above (10x max spread vs 9.7x/21.1x measured behavioral levers) says why.

=========================================================================
VARIANCE IS A COACHING SIGNAL, NOT A COMPLIANCE ONE
=========================================================================
The vendor's own distribution says the tail is where the money is: $13/active-day average,
"below $30 per active day for 90% of users" — i.e. a real spread, with a top decile above 2x
the average. A developer at 3x the median is EITHER your highest-leverage engineer (the L-2.2
champion doing the most valuable work) OR someone pasting 193KB files into prompts (the
measured 9.7x). **Same number, opposite response** — so investigate before you intervene, and
never turn it into an individual scorecard (L-5.3's Goodhart problem; the same discipline
L-3.3 applied to code churn).
CONTROLS: caps at org/group/user + a CHOSEN at-ceiling behaviour (Copilot states the choice
  explicitly: "Usage continues at published per-credit rates" OR "Usage is blocked until the
  next billing cycle" — l41 sheet). Rate limits sized from the per-user TPM/RPM table by org
  size (1-5 users 200k-300k TPM/user down to 500+ users 10k-15k) — that table is a budget-lab
  input, not just an ops detail.

=========================================================================
MODULE FRAMING
=========================================================================
Analogy: how a payer manages drug spend. Forecast total = members x utilization x cost per
  script (band 1+2). Handle SPECIALTY drugs separately because a tiny number of cases dominate
  the total (band 3, the lumpy items). Publish a FORMULARY that tiers by clinical need, not by
  price — with the failure mode every payer knows: force the cheapest option and you get worse
  outcomes and higher downstream cost when the cheap drug doesn't work and the patient lands
  in the ED. That maps to every beat, and "cheap drug fails" IS the $0.0368-and-wrong result.
Spine: a team budget is a forecast of BEHAVIOR, built in three bands — and the model policy
  on top of it should be written for CORRECTNESS, not cost, because the model barely moves the
  bill while it moves the error rate a lot.
Five moves: (1) three bands, not one number; (2) start from the public prior, replace it with
  your own pilot baseline; (3) the lumpy band scales with policy, so audit the L-03 switches;
  (4) route on "can you check the answer?", escalate on task type never on confidence, and
  remember effort is the finer dial than model tier; (5) set caps + a chosen ceiling behaviour,
  then read variance as a coaching signal.
