M-11.1 — Your baseline (capture cycle time, defects, rework — week 1, no AI)
Methodology module: no headless artifact by nature (like M-1.6 / M-4.1). The
"recording" is this dated, doc-verified definitions sheet. Verified 2026-07-25.

WHY THIS MODULE
  You cannot claim a delta without a BEFORE-reading. Week 1 = capture the no-AI
  baseline for one repeatable task class; M-11.3 re-runs the same class WITH the full
  toolkit and subtracts. A vendor's "40% faster" is marketing; the only productivity
  number that changes how YOU work is one you took yourself.

SCOPE (per BLUEPRINT — do not steal M-11.2/M-11.3)
  M-11.1 = baseline CAPTURE, three metrics (cycle time / escaped defects / rework).
  M-11.2 = the frameworks (DORA, SPACE, DX Core 4) + the vanity-metrics refuse-list
           (acceptance rate, LOC, %AI). M-11.1 only lightly teases those.
  M-11.3 = the delta + ANIM-11 personal dashboard.

THE LOAD-BEARING THESIS
  Baseline SPEED and QUALITY together. The documented AI-era risk is going faster while
  getting sloppier, so a speed-only baseline flatters you. Two of the three metrics are
  quality metrics on purpose.

DOC-VERIFIED DEFINITIONS (2026-07-25, with the hedges kept honest)
  CYCLE TIME (speed)
   - DORA "lead time for changes" (the neighbouring, canonical metric): "The amount of
     time it takes for a change to go from committed to version control to deployed in
     production." (dora.dev/guides/dora-metrics-four-keys)
   - "Cycle time" (tooling term, NOT a DORA metric): first commit -> released, subdivided
     coding/pickup/review/deploy (linearb.io/blog/cycle-time). HEDGE: no single canonical
     definition; start point varies by tool (first-commit vs ticket-start) -> module says
     "as commonly defined by engineering-metrics tools" and tells students to fix one start
     point so before/after are apples-to-apples.
  ESCAPED DEFECTS (quality)
   - "Defects that make it through your development and testing process and reach users in
     production"; broader: "any defect found after the development team has declared the
     work done." (plandek.com/blog/escaped-defects). HEDGE: reputable vendor, not a
     standards body; "commonly defined as". NOT bugs caught in dev (those = process working).
  REWORK / CHURN (quality)
   - GitClear churn definition: "For a line to qualify as 'churned,' it must have been
     authored, pushed to the git repo, and then reverted or substantially revised within
     the subsequent two weeks." Abstract: churn = "the percentage of lines that are reverted
     or updated less than two weeks after being authored."
   - AI link (GitClear's measured figures): 2021=3.6%, 2022=4.0%, 2023=5.5%, 2024 projected
     ~7.1%; "the rise of AI Assistants are strongly correlated with 'mistake code'."
   - HEDGE (stated in-module): GitClear is a commercial analytics vendor, not peer-reviewed;
     2024 is THEIR projection; correlation != causation (they say so). Cited as "GitClear's
     analysis" and "the shape is the warning," never as law. Newer 2025/26 figures NOT quoted
     (source 403'd; unverified).
  VANITY-METRIC ANCHOR (light tease; M-11.2 owns the deep cut)
   - SPACE paper (Forsgren/Storey/Maddila/Zimmermann/Houck/Butler, ACM Queue 2021), verbatim:
     "productivity cannot be reduced to a single dimension (or metric!)"; "Even straightforward
     metrics such as number of pull requests, commits, or code reviews are prone to errors...
     [activity metrics] should never be used in isolation." (queue.acm.org/detail.cfm?id=3454124)
   - Acceptance rate = engagement/adoption, not productivity (GitHub's framing; well-attested,
     but no single pinned verbatim GitHub/DX sentence extracted today -> anchored on SPACE).
  FRAMEWORK FRAMING (for M-11.2; one line each)
   - SPACE: "satisfaction and well-being; performance; activity; communication and
     collaboration; and efficiency and flow."
   - DX Core 4: "speed, effectiveness, quality, and business impact." (getdx.com/research)
  MEASUREMENT PITFALLS
   - Hawthorne effect: "When individuals modify an aspect of their behaviour in response to
     their awareness of being observed." (catalogofbias.org/biases/hawthorne-effect, Oxford CEBM).
     Relevance: timing yourself briefly inflates your numbers until novelty fades.
   - Small-n: stated in the module's own words (a solo, few-week baseline is a tiny non-random
     sample; high variance; one hard bug skews it -> "directional, not precise"). No crisp
     citable primary sentence found -> not quoted, hedged in prose.

ILLUSTRATIONS (real course timings, framed honestly)
  Used as "what a measured task looks like", NOT as the student's baseline (all course
  timings are WITH-AI runs): 90s -> 24s (M-7.1, a repo question without vs with a
  constitution) and 141s (M-10.5, issue -> PR). Module is explicit that the student's
  baseline is THEIR task class, measured WITHOUT AI.
