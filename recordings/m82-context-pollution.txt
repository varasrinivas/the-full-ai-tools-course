  *** ONE QUESTION. $1.12 of pure waste. Same answer either way. ***

=== THE MECHANISM (why 9.7x, not 1.3x) ===
 - PASTE: the whole file must be written to cache (cache_creation 103,192) and
   then RE-READ on every subsequent turn (cache_read 261,017 across 3 turns).
   Pasted content is not charged once — it is charged EVERY TURN it stays in
   context, which is the 1:17,765 carried-context effect from M-8.1 applied to
   something you chose to carry.
 - REFERENCE: the agent decides what it actually needs. It greps/reads
   selectively, so only the matching slice enters context. cache_creation was
   6,465 — about 6% of the paste's — because it never had to hold the file.

=== THE DETAIL THAT KILLS THE USUAL EXCUSE ===
  People paste to "save the agent a turn." The reference run used MORE turns
  (4 vs 3) and still cost ONE TENTH. A turn is worth ~$0.03 (M-8.1); the paste
  cost ~$1.12. You are spending a dollar to save three cents.

=== WHAT THIS MEANS IN PRACTICE ===
  Say "read seed.sql and tell me X", not "here is seed.sql: <193KB>".
  Say "the failing test output is in target/surefire-reports/X.txt", not a paste
  of the whole log. Give the PATH and let the agent choose the slice — it can
  grep, head, or read a range; a paste forecloses all of that and commits you to
  carrying the whole thing for the rest of the session.
  Corollary for long sessions: a paste made on turn 2 is still being paid for on
  turn 20. Pollution compounds; a path does not.

METHOD NOTE: the "paste" was simulated by piping the file into `claude -p`,
which is the closest faithful analogue (and avoids OS command-line length
limits). Both runs used --output-format json; figures are the raw usage
envelope. Steady-state caveat from M-7.3 does not apply here — neither run
edited a memory file, and the difference is 9.7x, far outside cache-warmth noise.
