THE FRESH CHECK, AND ITS RESULT (dora.dev/guides/dora-metrics-four-keys, 2026-07-29)
=========================================================================
WHY IT WAS RUN: L-5.1's spine is the speed/stability split, so the grouping had to be right.
  Going in, I believed m112-frameworks.txt had MIS-grouped "failed deployment recovery time"
  under throughput — reasoning from the classic four-keys mental model, where time-to-restore
  is a STABILITY metric. That hypothesis was WRONG and the earlier sheet was correct.
CONFIRMED CURRENT GROUPING — 5 metrics, 3 + 2, under DORA's own headings:
  THROUGHPUT (3)
   - Change lead time — "The amount of time it takes for a change to go from committed to
     version control to deployed in production."
   - Deployment frequency — "The number of deployments over a given period or the time
     between deployments."
   - Failed deployment recovery time — "The time it takes to recover from a deployment that
     fails and requires immediate intervention."   <-- UNDER THROUGHPUT, NOT STABILITY
  INSTABILITY (2)
   - Change fail rate — "The ratio of deployments that require immediate intervention
     following a deployment."
   - Deployment rework rate — "The ratio of deployments that are unplanned but happen as a
     result of an incident in production."
NOTE THE HEADING WORD: DORA's second group is labelled **"Instability"**, not "Stability."
  These are metrics you want LOW. A dashboard labelled "stability" with these two underneath
  it inverts the direction-of-good for anyone reading it quickly.
*** THE SECOND, SUBTLER STALENESS (new teaching point, discovered by this check) ***
  Everyone carrying the classic four-keys model places time-to-restore on the stability side.
  DORA's CURRENT grouping puts failed deployment recovery time under THROUGHPUT. So a leader
  is wrong twice over: the metric was RENAMED (MTTR -> failed deployment recovery time) AND
  RE-GROUPED. The rename is easy to notice; the regroup is not, and it silently changes what
  your "stability half" contains.
METHOD NOTE WORTH KEEPING: this is the second time a same-session check has been run against

  per-subagent-attribution finding had genuinely aged). Here the prior work held and MY

MODULE FRAMING
=========================================================================
Analogy: quality measurement across a provider network. No payer judges 200 practices on one
  blended score — it would describe no practice. You standardize the measure definition so
  "readmission" means the same thing everywhere, you read the DISTRIBUTION and go find the
  outliers, and you use it to decide where to send help rather than to publish a league table
  — because the moment practices are ranked they start coding to the measure instead of
  treating to it. Every clause maps to org-level DORA, and it is payer-native.
Spine: org-level DORA is not the team scorecard scaled up. Five metrics (3 throughput /
  2 instability, and recovery time is NOT where you think); AI pushes the halves in OPPOSITE
  directions so a throughput-only dashboard flatters you while instability grows; an org
  average describes no team; and ranking teams destroys the measurement.
Four moves: (1) fix the names AND the grouping — at org scale a stale definition propagates
  into every dashboard and comparison built on it; (2) read the halves apart, expecting them
  to diverge; (3) read the DISTRIBUTION, not the average — the metric is a pointer to where
  help goes; (4) never rank — and respond to bad instability with CONTROL SYSTEMS (DORA's own
  shopping list = T-06 tests, T-10 hooks/gates, L-3.3 review policy), not exhortation.
Goodhart lineage now explicit at three scales: L-3.3 churn (team), L-4.2 cost variance
  (individual), L-5.1 DORA (org) — same failure, escalating blast radius.
